# Writing that reads as a person, not a template

Read this only when drafting a summary, bullets or a letter. The lint tools (`lint_resume`, `lint_cover`, or `career build` in local mode) enforce the mechanical parts; this covers the judgment.

**Start from their words.** Each bullet is the atom's `say` lightly edited, in the register of the voice sample in `profile`. Do not upgrade it.
Move the posting's priority to the front and use its terminology only where the atom supports it. Polishing a plain sentence into a grand one is the fastest way to sound generated.

**Say the specific thing.** Name the system, the count, the date. If the atom has no number, write the bullet without one; a vague number is worse than none.

**Vary the shape.**
- Bullets differ in length (roughly 8 to 30 words); a couple are short.
- Not every bullet carries a metric, and not every one is "verb + object + result".
- Do not open two bullets in a role with the same word. Do not hang a result off the end with "...enabling / ensuring / resulting in".
- Avoid "A, B, and C" triplets as a default cadence.

**Summary:** at most two sentences, plain, no pronouns, no adjectives about themselves. A fact about what they do beats a claim about who they are.

**Cover letters** follow the same rules, plus: open with something specific to the company from the posting, never "I am writing to..." or "I am excited to...".
Do not retell the resume; pick the one or two atoms most relevant to this role and say why. Skip stock lines ("perfect fit", "aligns with my passion",
"thank you for your time and consideration"); end on a plain sentence. First person is fine here.

**Tense and punctuation:** past tense for past roles, present for the current one. Commas and periods, no dashes as punctuation.

**Words that mark generated text** (the linter flags these, and the list lives in `career.py`): spearheaded, leveraged, orchestrated, utilized, robust, seamless, cutting-edge,
proven track record, results-driven, passionate, dynamic, detail-oriented, responsible for, worked on, ensured, and similar filler.

Nothing guarantees a document cannot be spotted as AI-assisted, and AI detectors are unreliable, so do not chase them. What holds up is specific, true, first-hand detail, plus the user reading it once and changing anything that does not sound like them.
